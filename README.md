## Executive Summary – Top 3 Findings

The EDA helped identify a few clear patterns in customer churn:
1. Newer customers are more likely to leave. Churned customers had a median tenure of 10 months, compared with 18 months for customers who stayed. This suggests that the first year is an important period for customer retention.
2. Customers with more support issues are at higher risk. Churned customers had a higher ticket velocity (0.20) than retained customers (0.07). Frequent support requests may indicate that customers are facing problems with the service.
3. Contract type has a strong connection with churn. Month-to-month customers had an 85.39% churn rate, while customers on two-year contracts had a much lower churn rate of 37.39%.

## Methodology & Visualizations
I started by cleaning the dataset and handling missing values. I then explored the data using descriptive statistics and different visualizations to understand how factors such as tenure, contract type, support tickets, and charges are related to churn.
I also checked for outliers using the IQR method and used correlation analysis to understand relationships between numerical variables.
The visualizations helped make the churn patterns easier to identify and compare across different customer groups.


## Recommended Strategic Interventions
Based on the findings, the business should focus on improving the customer experience during the early months, especially through better onboarding and regular follow-ups.
Customers showing a sudden increase in support tickets should also be identified early so that problems can be resolved before they lead to churn.
Finally, the company can encourage month-to-month customers to move to longer-term contracts by offering suitable incentives and additional benefits. Customers with month-to-month contracts and no technical support should receive extra attention because they represent a particularly high-risk group.
